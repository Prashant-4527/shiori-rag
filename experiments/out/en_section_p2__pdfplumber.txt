(1) Economic and industrial cooperation since the 1980s
Among the ASEAN countries, some started to achieve economic development relatively early, in
the 1960s in the case of Singapore and in the 1980s in the case of Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand,
due to the successful development of export-oriented manufacturing industries introducing foreign
capital. At those times, Japan, which had already achieved postwar reconstruction and started to play
the role of a major economic power, expanded economic cooperation through official development aid
(ODA) and other means. Against the backdrop of major factors such as the yen’s appreciation due to
the Plaza Accord in 1985, Japanese manufacturing companies actively made FDIs in the ASEAN region
and developed production networks there in the second half of the 1980s and later.
At that time, Japan occupied a major position in the economies of ASEAN countries in terms of both
trade and investment. Figure II-3-5-1 shows changes in the shares of export destination and import
sources countries in ASEAN’s exports and imports. In 1980, Japan was the country with the largest
shares in both import and exports of ASEAN, 22.1% in imports and 29.5% in exports. Later, as triangular
trade, whereby Japan assembled intermediate goods in ASEAN countries for export to the United States,
Europe and other markets, developed on a global scale, Japan’s share as an export destination country
continued to decline, while its share as an import source increased to around a quarter of the total by the
middle of the 1990s. Afterwards, due to the development of a system of division of work within the
ASEAN region, Japan’s share as an import source also started to decline.
As for changes in FDIs, Japan consistently made FDIs in ASEAN countries from the 1970s onwards
and increased the value of FDIs steeply in the second half of the 1980s and later.344 After increasing to
nearly 8 billion dollars in 1997, when the Asian currency crisis occurred, the value of FDIs declined
slightly, but Japanese companies continued to make large amounts of FDIs in ASEAN countries (Figure
II-3-5-2). The continuous FDIs made over the long period of time contributed to the development of
ASEAN-wide production networks by Japanese companies.
